# Submission Quality Checklist

Run this checklist before considering a manuscript ready for submission.

## Story

- [ ] The central contribution is clear in one sentence.
- [ ] The title, abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion tell the same story.
- [ ] Each contribution is supported by a result, figure, table, or citation.
- [ ] The paper does not overstate novelty.

## Evidence

- [ ] Every numerical claim has a source.
- [ ] Every comparison names the baseline, metric, and evaluation setting.
- [ ] Every major claim appears in the claim-evidence map.
- [ ] Missing evidence is marked or resolved.

## Methods

- [ ] Dataset or materials are described clearly.
- [ ] Inclusion/exclusion criteria are stated when relevant.
- [ ] Preprocessing is reproducible.
- [ ] Model, algorithm, or experimental procedure is described in enough detail.
- [ ] Training or experiment settings are reported.
- [ ] Evaluation metrics are defined.
- [ ] Statistical tests and uncertainty estimates are described.
- [ ] Code/data availability is stated when needed.

## Results

- [ ] Results paragraphs answer specific experimental questions.
- [ ] Primary results are reported with uncertainty where possible.
- [ ] Negative or mixed results are not hidden.
- [ ] Results prose does not merely repeat tables.
- [ ] Interpretation is separated from observation.

## Figures And Tables

- [ ] Captions are standalone.
- [ ] Every panel is described.
- [ ] Axes, colors, symbols, and abbreviations are defined.
- [ ] Table notes define metrics and uncertainty.
- [ ] Figures and tables are cited in order.
- [ ] Visual claims match the Results text.

## Citations

- [ ] Citations are real and relevant.
- [ ] Claims with missing citations are marked `[CITATION NEEDED]`.
- [ ] "First", "novel", and "state-of-the-art" claims are verified.
- [ ] Related work is synthesized by theme.

## Discussion

- [ ] Principal findings are stated first.
- [ ] Results are compared with prior work.
- [ ] Alternative explanations are acknowledged.
- [ ] Limitations are specific.
- [ ] Future work follows from limitations.
- [ ] Conclusion does not introduce new claims.

## Language

- [ ] Causal language is justified by study design.
- [ ] Overconfident language is replaced with cautious scientific wording.
- [ ] Terminology is consistent.
- [ ] Abbreviations are defined.
- [ ] The manuscript is written in clear English.

## Reviewer Readiness

- [ ] Likely reviewer objections have been identified.
- [ ] Weak claims have been revised or removed.
- [ ] Missing experiments are acknowledged or completed.
- [ ] Reproducibility gaps are resolved or disclosed.
- [ ] The abstract does not promise more than the paper demonstrates.
